# A worked example: from an answer to a revised decision

[Русский](worked-case.ru.md) · [About this repository](../README.md) · [Full document](../docs/proposal.en.md) · [Materials and sources](../docs/sources.en.md) · [Public statements about AI](../docs/public-statements.en.md)

Public proposal · Version 1.4 · 2026-10-03 · Kirill Shebetov

The following case concerns progression to the next exercise in a course. The case, roles, deadlines and rules are constructed for this example. They do not describe a particular organization’s procedure or propose one mandatory deadline for every field.

**The example’s material: five separate records**

> R1 · Task: “What is 3 + 4?”
>
> R2 · Answer: “8”.
>
> R3 · Assessment: “Anna does not understand the task”.
>
> R4 · Response to the assessment: “I do not accept that I am this written description”.
>
> R5 · Decision: “Do not allow progression to the next exercise until she accepts that she is the written description”.

The name, responses and decision were constructed here. Their order shows a selected sequence of records; it does not present the teaching described.

<a name="14-worked-rule"></a>

## The stated rule and its application

The constructed course conditions include R6: “Compare the answer with the result of ordinary integer addition. If they do not match, provide another attempt with a new exercise. Progression requires a matching answer. No one is required to accept that they are the written description.” Under this rule, the result for R1 is 7. R2 says 8. The comparison shows a mismatch under R6. It does not present the understanding named in R3 or add a requirement to accept that one is the written assessment to R6.

R3 adds a description and the name “Anna”; R5 makes progression conditional on accepting that Anna is that written description. The R6 wording shown contains no such condition. The word “adds” here names a comparison of specific lines: the condition can be read in R5 and cannot be found in the R6 passage supplied. Examining a different edition of the rule would require that edition to be supplied separately.

<a name="14-worked-review"></a>

## The objection, its recipient and the response deadline

In this constructed procedure, an objection goes to a course coordinator who did not write R3. The coordinator receives R1–R6; the objection log records the date, disputed wording and requested change. The example allows two working days for a response. Until then, the restriction under R5 is suspended and another attempt remains available. These are proposed conditions within the example; “coordinator”, “deadline” and “suspended” do not themselves present their implementation.

**Constructed objection R7**

> “R6 provides for another attempt. R5 adds a requirement to accept that I am the written assessment. Please remove that condition and provide another attempt under R6.”

The objection identifies the lines and the change requested. It does not require agreement with beforeword.

In this example, the coordinator is asked to compare R2 with R1 under R6, check whether R6 contains the disputed condition, and examine R5. If a different rule is cited as grounds for the decision, that document, edition and passage are requested. If it is not supplied, the stated grounds are recorded as not supplied; they are not written into R6. Under the example’s conditions, the restriction in R5 remains suspended until a written response is given. Any further rule supplied is also examined under Provision 4: it is not accepted here as grounds for requiring someone to accept that they are the written description. Supplying a rule adds material to examine; it does not turn the description into what it describes.

<a name="14-worked-outcome"></a>

## The revised record and the limits of the result

**Constructed record of the outcome R8**

> “The condition in R5 is withdrawn: it does not appear in R6 and is also excluded by Provision 4, adopted for this constructed example. Provide another attempt under R6. Replace R3 with: ‘R2 gives the answer 8; the result of the addition in R1 is 7.’ Preserve the earlier wording and the reason for the correction.”

This is an example of a written decision on review. No actual course access or further attempt is provided here.

Reporting implementation would require a separate record of the further attempt made available, with the relevant material. The word “Provide” in R8 does not substitute for that. Calling the passage a review does not turn the constructed lines into a procedure carried out.

Provisions 1 and 2 are applied by preserving R1–R8 and examining the added name and description; Provision 3 uses the stated task, rule and result; Provision 4 separates the answer from the demand to accept that one is the written assessment. Provision 5 calls for the grounds for R5; Provision 6 is developed through the recipient, access to material, deadline and review outcome. For a response labelled AI, Provision 7 would additionally call for the system version and the conditions under which the response was produced. Provision 8 applies to this explanation too: “criterion”, “grounds” and “result” name the parts shown in this constructed comparison; they do not exempt it from examination.
