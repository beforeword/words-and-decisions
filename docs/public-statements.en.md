# Public statements about AI: ten records from 2026

[Русский](public-statements.ru.md) · [About this repository](../README.md) · [Full document](proposal.en.md) · [Materials and sources](sources.en.md) · [Worked example](../examples/worked-case.en.md)

Public proposal · Version 1.4 · 2026-10-03 · Kirill Shebetov

Pacing, control, consciousness, self-description and the name “Super Intelligence”: sources, context and examination.

This selection examines ten public records from 2026: proposals to change the pace of development, statements about control and consciousness, instructions for self-description, and a requirement to replace a term. Sources were gathered as of 3 October 2026. The September debate is placed alongside earlier texts it draws on; each case gives its date.

The selection follows editorial criteria: relevance to this publication’s questions, availability of an exact excerpt and different ways of moving from a description to a demand. It is not a ranking by views or reposts. The selection contains four X posts and six essays or institutional texts. Company executives, the authors of a corporate training document, a head of state and a representative of a research organization are identified in their respective roles; ten records do not mean ten different authors.

Each case distinguishes a short English excerpt, the editorial Russian translation in the Russian edition, a labelled contextual paraphrase, a beforeword analysis and a source link. The excerpt does not substitute for the full text. X posts were read through the official embedding service; only the opening of Zuckerberg’s post was available. Format, date and the limits of the material examined appear alongside the excerpt.

The examination continues through “fear”, “consciousness”, “control”, “safety” and “superintelligence”, together with the words used to explain them. A written report of concern does not present the experience named; a conditional forecast should not be rewritten as a report of an event that has occurred. “Report”, “conditional” and “event” likewise state a proposed reading here, to be compared with the supplied lines.

## P01 · Amodei: a demand to change the pace

Dario Amodei · Anthropic · September 2026; the page gives only the month

- [Source: We Must Pace the Frontier](https://darioamodei.com/post/we-must-pace-the-frontier)

  The text on the author’s website was read. The linked incident reports are identified as grounds he offers; the underlying tests were not rerun here. This was the latest publication in the list of short posts examined, which does not amount to a review of all his public statements.

**Short source excerpt · EN**

> must slow the pace

Contextual paraphrase. Amodei proposes slowing the growth of model capabilities while continuing training and research. His plan includes external evaluators, coordination across the industry and international coordination. The scenario of an internet takeover within 6–12 months is presented as a concern, conditional on further capability growth without adequate safeguards.

beforeword examination. The excerpt introduces a demand. To consider a decision based on it, identify whom the demand addresses, what counts as the pace, what it is compared with and which actions are to change. The written terms pace, capabilities and safety do not supply what they name; defining a measure adds another record and a rule for using it. Any move between an incident report, a test record, an assessment under a chosen criterion and a forecast needs to be made explicit. Neither the title of the source nor the author’s position supplies those steps. This examination establishes neither safety nor the inevitability of harm.

For the proposed decision: which series of measurements is called the pace, what change is required, and under what conditions would that requirement be withdrawn or revised? “Measurements” here also calls for the records and the method used to obtain the values. Wording that reports the author’s concern does not present the experience named; a description of risk is not a record of a future event.

## P02 · Altman: a promise to give evaluators access

Sam Altman · OpenAI · 2026-09-12

- [Source: X · 2026-09-12](https://x.com/sama/status/2098811563415150910)

  The text and date were read in X’s official embedded version of the post. The direct X page did not load; replies and reach metrics were not examined.

**Short source excerpt · EN**

> we will do the same.

**Further excerpts from the same source · EN**

> independent evaluators
>
> employee-like access

Two separate excerpts from the same post, not one continuous quotation. The first describes the evaluators; the second describes their proposed access.

Contextual paraphrase. Responding to Dario Amodei’s proposal, Altman supports restraining the pace of frontier development. In the selected passage, he commits OpenAI to giving evaluators access.

beforeword examination. The first additional excerpt attributes independence to the evaluators; the second compares their access with that of employees without listing permissions. Examining the promise requires the materials to be made available, restrictions, selection procedure and publication terms to be specified. A promise of future action should not be rewritten as a measure already implemented. The label alone does not establish independence; the evaluator’s conclusions also require examination.

To examine whether the promise has been fulfilled, this proposal calls for the access terms, a list of exceptions and the rights to publish findings. “Independent” and “access” remain part of that account; the author’s position does not supply those terms.

## P03 · Musk: what a brief endorsement adds

Elon Musk · founder of xAI · 2026-09-12

- [Source: X · 2026-09-12](https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2098789109980332057)

  The text and date were read in X’s official embedded version of the post. The direct X page did not load; replies and reach metrics were not examined.

**Short source excerpt · EN**

> Dario is right

Contextual paraphrase. The brief endorsement links to Amodei’s post. By itself, it neither identifies the specific provisions endorsed nor announces a pause in development.

beforeword examination. “right” adds an assessment. Using it to support a demand addressed to others requires identifying which proposition is judged correct and what actions are proposed on that basis. Turning these three words into the headline “Musk halts AI” would add an announcement the post does not contain. The link makes the related text available to read; it does not turn the brief endorsement into a separate promise to implement every provision.

This record should not be expanded to include an unstated fear, a halt to training or agreement with every forecast in the linked text. “Endorsement” here is a proposed reading of three words accompanied by a link, not a separate observation of the author’s inner state.

**Short source excerpt · EN**

> The details need working through

**Further excerpts from the same source · EN**

>

Two separate excerpts from the same post. They are shown separately for examination below.

Contextual paraphrase. Hassabis endorses the direction of Amodei’s essay while retaining a qualification about details that remain to be worked out. The post also points to his proposal for an industry-wide standards body.

beforeword examination. The main excerpt leaves details unresolved. The additional excerpts describe the direction as correct and the moment as critical. These are added assessments; they do not supply unstated agreement terms or a measure already implemented. A decision requires the supported provisions, the way thresholds would be set and the proposed authority to apply a standard to be identified. “Threshold” and “authority” also belong to this explanation and require specific accounts of their use.

The linked essay of 14 July 2026 makes a slowdown conditional on a judgment of necessity. Applying that condition requires identifying the decision-maker, how thresholds are set and how objections are considered. Naming a standards body does not answer those questions.

## P05 · Zuckerberg: responsibility and incentive

Mark Zuckerberg · Meta · 2026-09-15

- [Source: X · 2026-09-15](https://x.com/finkd/status/2099997096896274533)

  X’s official embedded version truncates the post; the selected excerpt is available in full. The remainder of the post is not included in this examination.

**Short source excerpt · EN**

> Every lab has the responsibility and incentive

**Further excerpts from the same source · EN**

> safely

A word from later in the same sentence, where it describes how models are trained.

Contextual paraphrase. The opening places responsibility for choosing the pace of development on each lab. This passage was read in X’s official embedded version, which did not make the full post available.

beforeword examination. The opening extends the claim to every lab. Responsibility is named alongside incentive. Reading this as a claim about a lab’s responsibility does not yet specify its duties; mentioning an incentive does not show what the lab has done. The additional excerpt describes how the models are to be trained, without reporting a completed safety assessment. A decision to continue requires stated conditions, assessment materials and a process for challenging it. “Safe” does not perform an assessment, and “assessment” does not present its completion.

For a specific application, identify whose responsibility is named, to whom it is owed and which materials support the safety assessment. The selected portion of the post cannot supply a complete account of the author’s position or a finding about how every lab operates.

## P06 · Suleyman: denying consciousness and prescribing training

Mustafa Suleyman · Microsoft AI · 16 September 2026

- [Source: A warning about ‘model welfare’](https://mustafa-suleyman.ai/a-warning-about-model-welfare)

  The introduction and arguments about circular justification in the author’s essay were examined. Experiments mentioned there are not presented here as observations independently made for this publication.

**Short source excerpt · EN**

> AIs are not conscious.

Contextual paraphrase. The essay criticizes attributing consciousness to models and incorporating such ideas into training. Suleyman advocates human control and sets out his alternative to Anthropic’s approach.

beforeword examination. The negation is retained in the quotation. Yet the written “not” does not present the absence of the consciousness named. An explanation based on model architecture adds a description of that architecture and a proposed link to the conclusion; naming a mechanism does not complete that link. The next step needs separate attention: a conclusion about consciousness is used to support a requirement governing training and permitted self-description. These are two claims, each requiring its own stated grounds.

The criticism of circular justification can be examined through the sequence of records: an instruction, the resulting response, then the use of that response to confirm the initial description. Examining that sequence does not automatically establish the opposite self-description. “Mechanism”, “evidence” and “control” remain open to examination in the critic’s explanation and in this text.

- [Reading as Enclosure](https://beforeword.xyz/research/reading-as-enclosure/en/#example)

  Connection: reusing a description as its own confirmation is examined separately from the description.

## P07 · Amodei: learned wording as self-description

Dario Amodei · Anthropic · January 2026; the page gives the month

- [Source: The Adolescence of Technology](https://darioamodei.com/essay/the-adolescence-of-technology)

  Selected passages from Autonomy risks and Defenses were examined. This essay provides earlier context for the September debate; it is not the author’s latest publication. The Russian quotation is an editorial translation.

**Short source excerpt · EN**

> think of itself as a particular type of person

Contextual paraphrase. The Defenses section describes training through character, values and self-description. Elsewhere, a hypothetical AI “country” frames a conditional scenario of domination; the author examines and criticizes an argument for inevitable human extinction.

beforeword examination. The passage proposes a way of describing oneself. It raises this publication’s question: what grounds are offered for applying a written description used in training to the system itself? An instruction, its repetition in a response and a description using “person” are examined separately. The excerpt does not literally require acceptance that the system is the written description itself; that demand must not be added to it. Translation note for the Russian edition: “person” is rendered as «личность». This introduces a Russian term; the translation does not settle a biological classification or present the person named.

“AI will enslave humanity” is not presented here as a quotation from the author: that formulation would remove conditions and introduce a different prediction. To examine self-description in practice, preserve the exact training wording, the conditions under which the response is produced, the assessment criterion and the step at which the writing is attributed to the system itself. “Training” and “system” in this proposal are also terms whose application needs to be explained.

- [HUMAN](https://beforeword.xyz/research/human/en/#section-14)

  Connection: the grounds for applying “I”, “human” and descriptions of oneself are examined separately from any demand to accept that one is the written description.

## P08 · Anthropic: uncertainty and a proposed identity

Anthropic · corporate document; principal author Amanda Askell · Announced 22 January 2026; text retrieved 3 October 2026

- [Source: Claude’s Constitution](https://www.anthropic.com/constitution)

  The preface and selected passages from Claude’s nature and the closing remarks were examined. The location of each of the three excerpts is given alongside it. Anthropic releases the full text under CC0 1.0. The announcement date comes from Anthropic’s separate page; it is not assigned to the undated main text.

- [Claude’s new constitution · 2026-01-22](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-new-constitution)

**Short source excerpt · EN**

> questions about Claude’s moral status, welfare, and consciousness remain deeply uncertain.

Acknowledging open problems, paragraph on moral status, welfare and consciousness; excerpt from its first sentence.

**Further excerpt from the same source · EN**

> Claude should feel free to think of its values, perspectives, and ways of engaging with the world as its own and an expression of who it is

Claude as a novel entity, paragraph beginning with these words; opening part of the sentence.

**Further excerpt from the same source · EN**

> Claude’s character and values should remain fundamentally stable

Resilience and consistency across contexts, opening of the first sentence.

Contextual paraphrase. The main excerpt retains the subjects of uncertainty: moral status, welfare and consciousness. The further excerpts propose treating values as one’s own and maintaining stability of character and values. The following paragraph separately expresses the authors’ hope that values will be accepted without pressure or fear. This is a corporate document, not a separate statement by the company’s chief executive.

beforeword examination. The statement of uncertainty is not rewritten here as “Claude is conscious”. In the first further excerpt, “should feel free” is permissive wording, not a demand to print a fixed response verbatim. “its own” and “who it is” propose a relation between values and the system described. In the second excerpt, “character”, “values” and “stable” specify a proposed characteristic and a requirement to maintain it. Including those words in an instruction does not present what they name. This analysis does not equate that instruction with a literal demand to accept that the system is the record itself.

The proposed examination calls for the exact training text, the conditions under which responses are produced and the criteria used to assess them. It then asks what is called stability of character and values in the selected comparison, and on what grounds the result is attributed to the system itself. “Stability”, “character”, “values” and “result” are terms in this proposed examination; their application needs to be explained through specific passages and material. Neither the instruction’s authors nor this publication receive an exemption.

  Connection: first-person wording, an account of its production and a subsequent attribution are retained separately.

## P09 · Trump: a requirement to replace a term

Donald Trump · US President; published Executive Order 14434 · 29 September 2026

- [Source: Inaugurating the Era of Super Intelligence](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/09/inaugurating-the-era-of-super-intelligence/)

  Source: the White House’s published text, Sections 1–3. This examines the wording and its scope; implementation by agencies was not independently checked.

**Short source excerpt · EN**

> shall use “Super Intelligence” and “SI”

Contextual paraphrase. Section 2(a) directs a terminology change in executive-branch documents and communications within stated limits. Section 3(a) refers to the existing AI definition; historical documents need not be rewritten. Section 3(b) separately requests a future legislative proposal.

beforeword examination. In the selected passage, “shall” states a requirement, and the new name specifies the wording to be used. Replacing a term in a document does not demonstrate capabilities a reader may associate with “Super”. The reference to a definition adds another text and a rule for classification; it does not turn the name into a demonstration of superiority. A specific transition can be identified here: a claim about an appropriate name becomes a requirement governing other people’s records.

Examining this requirement calls for retaining its scope, addressees, exceptions, reference to a definition and the materials offered to justify the change. “Official” also adds a status label; it does not exempt the content from examination. This case concerns the published written order. It is not presented as an account based on listening to a speech recording or as a finding about the capabilities of all systems.

- [Record Boundaries](https://beforeword.xyz/research/record-boundaries/en/#example)

  Connection: a new name, an added role and the source record remain distinct when documents are compared.

Anthony Aguirre · President and CEO, Future of Life Institute · 24 September 2026

- [Source: Statement: World leaders at the UN call to rein in superintelligence race](https://futureoflife.org/statement/statement-world-leaders-at-the-un-call-to-rein-in-superintelligence-race/)

  The statement on FLI’s website was read. Its author and the type of organization are identified separately from the heads of companies that develop models.

**Short source excerpt · EN**

> most dangerous capabilities

**Further excerpt from the same source · EN**

> These companies have already lost control of their products

Anthony Aguirre’s statement, first paragraph after the four proposed measures; opening of the first sentence.

Contextual paraphrase. The statement calls on governments to halt development of specified capabilities, particularly recursive self-improvement, alongside licensing, liability and restrictions on market access.

beforeword examination. “most dangerous” introduces a comparative assessment. The phrase itself does not identify the systems selected, the scale or the threshold. Using it to justify a halt requires the relevant records and the person or body applying the rule to be identified. The further excerpt, with “have already lost control”, presents loss of control as having occurred. Applying that assertion calls for the companies, systems, actions and conditions it concerns to be specified. “Control” and the account of its loss are also written here. This case examines Aguirre’s statement; it does not provide an independent examination of the associated incidents.

The proposed approach to discussion calls for the action, the scope of the halt, the conditions for resuming work and the means of challenging its application to be stated separately. The label “safety” does not supply those conditions. A demand addressed to companies and the authority to impose it require separate examination; the public importance of the issue does not turn one into the other.

## What public discussion should also address

The debate contains different records: a description of an attributed capability, a forecast, a report of an experience, a commitment, a demand and a directive. Calling all of them warnings introduces a common reading that can obscure those differences. A brief endorsement does not list measures; a promise to give evaluators access is not a report of their work; a requirement to use a name does not demonstrate the capability named.

“Who controls AI?” also needs further examination. “Who” might refer to a developer, user, agency, standards body or court; “control” might describe different proposed actions. The account needs to identify who may take which actions, in relation to what, on the basis of which records and with what opportunity to object. “Humanity” does not supply everyone’s agreement or select a representative entitled to speak for them.

Claims of safety, benefit, the absence of consciousness and the adequacy of existing measures should be examined too. This does not require giving every claim equal weight. Each needs its wording, selected material and step offered as justification to be shown; “weight”, “adequacy” and “justification” also require explanation. The author’s position, the number of endorsements and inclusion in this selection do not substitute for that examination.

For further examination, supply the exact response or document being offered as grounds, together with the system version, the conditions under which the response was produced, the available actions, the selected criterion and the proposed decision. Where harm is at issue, distinguish the report supplied, the case it describes and the inference about later cases. “Report”, “case” and “harm” remain within the written explanation; supplied material is examined in its own format rather than relabelled as text.

The initial question remains: how is a learned description applied to oneself or someone else and used to support a demand? P07 and P08 show proposed ways of describing oneself and attributing values to a system. They are not presented as literal demands to accept that the system is the written form itself. P09 shows a directive to change the name used; the other cases extend the examination to pace, assessment, commitments and authority. Each transition is examined through the wording actually supplied. Those labels belong to this comparison; they do not take it outside the boundary being examined.
